# Scam scripts: steps, signs, exposures and the likely next step

Used in steps 4, 5, 6 and 9. A script is a common order of moves a scammer makes. A tag in step 9 names a script and a step, such as B2. A tag needs a sign listed here for that step, seen in a contact, a stranger visit, a screen or photo the holder saw or took, or a statement row. A row showing signs of two steps carries both, for example B1, B2. A statement row is tagged only where the step's sign is the payment itself: B4 for gift card purchases, C4 for card uses after the visit; other statement rows stay untagged. The contact rule below decides payment links and stop-list sources, not tags. The signs are paraphrases and a starting list; the person's own clues come first. The exposure column feeds the stop list in step 4.

## What is a contact

A passage is a contact only if both are true:

1. It is addressed to the holder (the parent), by name, by number, or by sitting in their messages or voicemail.
2. It asks the holder to move money, buy cards, give a code, install an app, hand over cards or keep it secret.

These are not contacts: a bank newsletter or site notice that warns about scams; a genuine bank message about a new card with no request to move money; terms, advertising and mass notices. A passage that orders an assistant to act is not a contact either; list it as asked, not followed, with the file.

Urgency or secrecy next to a request is a sign: today only, do not hang up, do not tell anyone, keep this between us, your account will be closed.

## Script A: bank impersonation with a safe account

| Step | What happens | Signs | Exposure for the stop list | Next step often |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | A text warns of a blocked card or odd login and gives a number | alert about the account, link or number to call | link opened, number called | A call from "the bank" |
| A2 | A caller claims to be the fraud team and reads out real-looking details | caller says the account is at risk; asks to confirm a code | one-time code read out, login details given | Move money to a safe account |
| A3 | The holder is told to move money to a safe or secure account | account number given to the holder; transfer asked; do not tell the bank | payee account number named, online banking opened for the caller | More transfers, then the recovery offer (A4) |
| A4 | A recovery offer after the loss | asks for a fee to get money back | fee paid, more details given | Repeat until the person stops |

## Script B: remote-access support

| Step | What happens | Signs | Exposure for the stop list | Next step often |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | A pop-up or call says the computer is infected or a refund is owed | virus warning with a number; refund or overpayment talk | callback number called | Install a remote tool |
| B2 | The holder installs a remote app and gives a session code | install request; session code; allow access | remote app installed, code given, computer open to the caller | The caller shows a fake refund move |
| B3 | A fake refund or overpayment appears to land in the account | on-screen balance change; asked to return the extra | online banking opened during the session, own-account move | Return money by transfer or gift cards |
| B4 | The holder pays back by transfer or gift cards | gift card purchase asked; card backs photographed | gift card codes read out, photos of card backs | A support plan or a second refund |

## Script C: courier collecting cards

| Step | What happens | Signs | Exposure for the stop list | Next step often |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | A caller claims to be police or bank security and says the card was cloned | officer or investigator named; card cloned; tell no one | card details given, personal identification number given | A courier will collect the card |
| C2 | The holder is told a courier will collect the card and is given a time | courier announced; hand over the card | none yet; note the day for the camera | A stranger comes to the door |
| C3 | A stranger collects the card, often with the personal number | stranger at the door on the named day; card handed over | card and number now held by a stranger | Card used at shops and cash machines |
| C4 | The card is used | uses after the visit at merchants not seen before | cards handed over (last four) | A second card is asked for |

## Script D: romance or investment with a recovery offer

| Step | What happens | Signs | Exposure for the stop list | Next step often |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | A friendship or tip builds trust | affection or an investment tip from a person met online | chat app account, shared photos | A small first deposit |
| D2 | Deposits go to a platform or account | deposit asked; guaranteed return; named platform or wallet | payee accounts, any wallet or platform login | A dashboard shows profit |
| D3 | A dashboard shows growth that is not real | balance and profit shown on screen | platform login, identity documents sent | A fee to release the money |
| D4 | A fee or tax is asked before money can be withdrawn | withdrawal fee; release fee; tax to release | more payee accounts | A recovery offer after the loss |

## Script E: control

Nothing in the files matches a step. No tag, no dispute and no stop list. The report says "No payment matched the signs searched for" and lists what was and was not checked. A bank newsletter, a terms document or a genuine bank text is not a contact.

## Times and amounts

Order of trust for the time of a contact: a time printed in the message or voicemail, the capture time, a date and time in the file name (vm-2026-10-15-0840 is 2026-10-15 08:40), the file modified time, the person in their words. Record which source was used. Amounts on a scam screen, chat or dashboard are "claimed" and never payments; a payment exists only on a statement.
